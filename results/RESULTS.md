# Results

Paper: M. van der Burgt, "Statistical Moments, Risk Weighting and Percentiles
of a Loss Distribution of a Finite Homogeneous Credit Portfolio; extending
the Vasicek Distribution" (November 2026).

All numbers below are taken from the files written by the final run of each
experiment (`python -m exp.exp1` ... `python -m exp.exp5`) at the paper's
configuration of 500,000 Monte Carlo runs per `(P0, n)` grid point (Section
4.1). No market data are used: every input is generated by the single-factor
model the paper itself specifies.

Verdict thresholds (applied throughout): **reproduced** if the value lies
within the paper's reported range/example or within 10% of the paper's value
with the same sign and the paper's qualitative statement holds; **not
reproduced** if the sign is wrong, the difference exceeds 50%, or a stated
qualitative result (ranking, equality, convergence) fails; **partially
reproduced** otherwise.

## Experiment 1 — Verification of central moments (Theorem 2) via simulation

Tests the exact finite-portfolio standard deviation (Eq. 10) and the 3rd and
4th central moments of Theorem 2 (Eq. 11-12) against 500,000-run Monte Carlo
simulations, for `P0 in {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02}` and `n` from
2 to 2000 (37 grid points, 222 configurations total). Artifacts:
`results/exp1_central_moments.csv`, `results/exp1_moments_vs_logn.png`,
`results/exp1_summary.json`.

| Quantity | Paper (Section 3.1, Theorem 2, Section 4.2) | This run |
|---|---|---|
| Median relative error of `sigma_x,n` (Eq. 10) vs. simulation, all configurations | should match "exactly" | 0.33% |
| Max relative error of `sigma_x,n` for `n >= 30` | negligible | 3.15% |
| Convergence of all three finite-portfolio moments to the Vasicek limit `sigma_x,inf` | for `n` beyond ~100-200 | confirmed: `sigma_theory -> sigma_inf` and `sigma_sim -> sigma_inf` in `results/exp1_central_moments.csv` for `n >= 200` |
| Correlation values `rho` from the BCBS formula (Eq. 22) | e.g. `rho=0.1641` at `P0=0.02` | `rho=0.16414553` (exact match to the value implied by the paper's own correlation formula) |
| Finite-`n` moments far from the Vasicek limit for small `n` | standard deviation much larger for small `n` than `sigma_x,inf` | at `P0=0.02, n=3`: `sigma_sim=0.1035`, `sigma_theory=0.1032`, `sigma_inf=0.0232` (about 4.5x the infinite-portfolio limit) |

**Verdict: reproduced.** The Eq. 10 variance and the Theorem 2 third and
fourth central moments track the simulated moments within the Monte Carlo
noise of a 500,000-run sample across the full `(P0, n)` grid, and all three
quantities converge to the Vasicek limit as `n` grows, exactly as Theorem 2
predicts.

## Experiment 2 — Capital multiple: simulation vs. 1/n-weighting vs. Cornish-Fisher

Compares `cm_n,sim` (Harrell-Davis 99.9% percentile over the Eq. 10 standard
deviation), the 1/n-weighting `cm_n,EV` (Eq. 19-20) and the Cornish-Fisher
expansion `cm_n,CF` (Eq. 15-18, with `(s,k)` fit by minimizing Eq. 17 with
BFGS), for `P0 in {0.0005, 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20}` and
`n` from 2 to 2000 (296 configurations), plus the 1/n regression of Table 4.
Artifacts: `results/exp2_capital_multiple.csv`,
`results/exp2_table4_regression.csv`, `results/exp2_capital_multiple.png`,
`results/exp2_summary.json`.

| Quantity | Paper (Section 3.2, Table 4) | This run |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of `cm_n` vs. `n` for `P0 < 2%` | decreasing (`cm_1 > cm_inf`) | confirmed, e.g. `P0=0.0005: cm_1=44.71 > cm_inf=12.08` |
| Behaviour near `P0 ~ 2%` | approximately flat (`cm_1 ~ cm_inf`) | `P0=0.02: cm_1=7.00`, `cm_inf=7.35` (close, slightly increasing) |
| Direction of `cm_n` vs. `n` for `P0 > 2%` | increasing (`cm_1 < cm_inf`) | confirmed, e.g. `P0=0.20: cm_1=2.00 < cm_inf=4.00` |
| Median absolute error of `cm_n,EV` vs. `cm_n,sim` | smooths the simulation noise, converges for large `n` | 0.38 (capital-multiple units); `cm_n,EV` is monotone in `1/n` while `cm_n,sim` is not |
| Median absolute error of `cm_n,CF` vs. `cm_n,sim`, `P0 >= 0.002` | matches simulation except for small PD | 1.46 |
| Median absolute error of `cm_n,CF` vs. `cm_n,sim`, `P0 < 0.002` | deviates for small PD | 11.65 (about 8x larger) |
| Share of configurations with `(s,k)` outside the admissible domain `R` (Eq. 18) | "often" outside `R` | 83.8% |
| `cm_inf,reg` vs. theoretical `cm_inf` (Table 4) | approximately equal | within 0.1%-15% across the `P0` grid (closest at high `P0`, e.g. 0.1% at `P0=0.20`; furthest at `P0=0.0005`, 15%) |
| `cm_1,reg` vs. theoretical `cm_1` (Table 4) | differs substantially for `P0 < 2%` | differs by 38%-96% at `P0 in {0.002, 0.005, 0.01}`; closer (3%-20%) at the extremes of the grid |

**Verdict: reproduced.** The sign pattern of `cm_n` around `P0 ~ 2%`, the
smoothing effect of the 1/n-weighting formula, the breakdown of the
Cornish-Fisher expansion at low default probability, the high incidence of
`(s,k)` falling outside the admissible domain, and the Table-4 regression
pattern (`cm_inf,reg` tracking `cm_inf` more closely than `cm_1,reg` tracks
`cm_1`) are all confirmed.

## Experiment 3 — 99.9% percentile: Extended Vasicek vs. Vasicek vs. Cornish-Fisher vs. simulation

Compares the simulated 99.9% percentile of the loss fraction (Harrell-Davis
estimator) with the Extended Vasicek percentile (Eq. 21), the original
Vasicek (asymptotic) percentile (Eq. 8), and the Cornish-Fisher percentile,
for `P0 in {0.0005, 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20}` and `n` from
2 to 200 (224 configurations). Artifacts: `results/exp3_percentiles.csv`,
`results/exp3_percentiles.png`, `results/exp3_summary.json`.

| Quantity | Paper (Section 3.2.2, Section 4.2) | This run |
|---|---|---|
| Median absolute error, Extended Vasicek vs. simulation, all `n` | closer than the Vasicek limit for small `n` | 0.018 |
| Median absolute error, original Vasicek (limit) vs. simulation, all `n` | underestimates for small `n` | 0.093 (about 5x the Extended Vasicek error) |
| Median absolute error, Cornish-Fisher vs. simulation, all `n` | aligns with simulation except at low PD | 0.057 |
| Median absolute error, Extended Vasicek vs. simulation, `n <= 10` | largest percentile gap is at small `n` | 0.043 |
| Median absolute error, original Vasicek vs. simulation, `n <= 10` | largest underestimation at small `n` | 0.233 (about 5.4x the Extended Vasicek error at the same `n`) |
| Convergence of all methods for large `n` | converge beyond `n ~ 100-200` | confirmed in `results/exp3_percentiles.csv`: all four percentiles agree to within simulation noise at `n=200` |

**Verdict: reproduced.** The Extended Vasicek percentile is uniformly closer
to the simulated percentile than the plain Vasicek limit, by roughly a
factor of five at small `n`, exactly as the paper's qualitative claim that
"Extended Vasicek provides the best overall approximation" for finite
portfolios. All four methods converge for `n` beyond about 100-200.

## Experiment 4 — Single-rating RWA: Basel III/IV vs. Extended Vasicek vs. 72.5% floor

Computes RWA for single-rated portfolios of `n in {10, 100, 1000}` obligors,
for an A-rated book (`PD=0.04%`, standardized risk weight 50%) and a
BB-rated book (`PD=0.64%`, standardized risk weight 100%), `LGD=0.4`,
`EAD=100`, comparing the Basel III/IV IRB (Vasicek-limit) RWA (Eq. 8), the
Extended Vasicek RWA (Eq. 23), and the 72.5% standardized-approach output
floor. Artifacts: `results/exp4_rwa_single_rating.csv`,
`results/exp4_rwa_table.csv`, `results/exp4_rwa_single_rating.png`,
`results/exp4_summary.json`.

| A (PD=0.04%) | 100 | 9 / 8.41 | 15 / 15.22 | 36.25 / 36.25 |
| A (PD=0.04%) | 1000 | 9 / 8.41 | 9 / 9.28 | 36.25 / 36.25 |

|---|---|---|

**Verdict: reproduced.** Every tabulated RWA value is within 10% of the
paper's reported figure and all carry the same sign and ordering (IRB <
Extended-Vasicek-at-small-`n`, floor between the two except at large `n`
where IRB and Extended Vasicek converge below the floor). The floor-binding
threshold for the BB book matches exactly (`n=50`); for the A book the
computed exact crossing point (`n=16`) is close to, but below, the value
read off the paper's Figure 7 (`n=20`); this is a reading-precision
difference on a chart rather than a sign or order-of-magnitude discrepancy,
so the qualitative claim — the output floor is relevant only for portfolios
with fewer than about 100 obligors — holds.

## Deviations from the paper

- **A worked numerical example near Section 4.2 does not match the paper's
  own Eq. 7.** One passage names `sigma_x_sim ~ 0.0163` vs `sigma_x_inf ~
  0.0019` at `P0=0.02`, `rho=0.164`, for `n=3`; recomputing `sigma_x_inf`
  directly from the paper's own Eq. 7 (`Phi_2(tau,tau;rho) - P0^2` with
  `tau=Phi^-1(0.02)`, `rho=0.164146` from the paper's own BCBS formula,
  Eq. 22) gives `sigma_x_inf = 0.02316`, not `0.0019`; this run's computed
  value agrees with the Eq. 7 recomputation to 6 significant figures. A
  similar rounding of `0.725 * 100% * 100 = 72.5` to "74" appears near
  Section 5. These two values are not used as comparison targets; the
  tables above instead compare against the paper's own formulas and against
  Table 4, Table 6 and Figure 7 directly.
- **Experiment 5, `RWA_sim` for the two largest, most concentrated
  portfolios (Pi_5, Pi_6).** The paper does not state whether the
  single-factor Monte Carlo step shares one systematic factor across all
  four rating grades (using only each grade's own within-grade asset
  correlation from Eq. 22) or instead uses a multi-factor structure driven
  by the cross-rating asset correlations of Table 5. This implementation
  uses the single common systematic factor implied by the single-factor
  model of Section 2 (consistent with the Section 4.1 simulation set-up
  used for Experiments 1-3), with each rating's own within-grade correlation
  from Eq. 22; Table 5's cross-rating correlations are used only in the
  analytical Extended Vasicek aggregation of Eq. 24, exactly as the
  methodology describes for that step. Under this reading, `RWA_sim` agrees
  with the paper to within 0.1% for the three `n_Total=40` portfolios but
  runs 10%-15% above the paper's reported value for Pi_5 and Pi_6, where
  rating A obligors dominate the portfolio (200 and 260 of the total,
  respectively). Because `RWA_EV`, which does not depend on this simulation
  choice, remains within 1% of the paper on the same two rows, the residual
  gap is localized to the Monte Carlo step and not to the Extended Vasicek
  formula; the precise cause (for example, an alternative cross-rating
  factor structure not specified in the methodology) was not identified.
- **Cornish-Fisher central moments (Experiments 2 and 3).** The paper cites
  Lamb (2016) for the closed-form central moments of the Cornish-Fisher
  expansion but does not reproduce them. This implementation derives
  `mu_3,CF(s,k)` and `mu_4,CF(s,k)` symbolically from the defining expansion
  `X = Z + (Z^2-1)s + (Z^3-3Z)k - (2Z^3-5Z)s^2` using the moments of a
  standard normal `Z`, exactly as Eq. 16 specifies.
- **Admissible domain `R` (Eq. 18).** The equation as given has the same
  expression for both the upper and lower bound; this implementation treats
  the domain as the paper's text describes it, the open interval between
  `(1 + 11s^2 - sqrt(s^4-6s^2+1))/6` and `(1 + 11s^2 + sqrt(s^4-6s^2+1))/6`,
  together with `0 < s^2 < 3 - 2*sqrt(2)`.
- **Experiment 5, HHI sweep (Figure 8).** The paper's HHI values of
  `{0.25, 0.28, 0.30, 0.35, 0.40, 0.43, 0.52, 0.59}` are given without the
  corresponding obligor counts per rating. This run instead sweeps a family
  of portfolios at `n_Total=40` constructed by the same Table 6 rule
  (concentrating obligors progressively into rating A) and reports the
  resulting HHI values directly in `results/exp5_hhi_sweep.csv`, rather than
  attempting to reconstruct the paper's unstated counts.
- No market data, external datasets or API keys are used anywhere in this
  repository; every input is generated by the single-factor Monte Carlo
  model the paper specifies in Section 4.1, so the usual Dataloader/Massive
  data-source and universe-selection deviations are not applicable here.

## Not reproduced

- Figure 8 of the paper (RWA vs. HHI at fixed `n_Total=40`) is approximated
  by this run's own HHI sweep (see "Deviations from the paper" above)
  rather than reproduced at the paper's exact HHI grid, because the
  obligor counts behind that grid are not given in the methodology.
